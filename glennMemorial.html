The time has come, O Lord

The time has come, O Lord, for us to leave this place
Guide us and protect us and keep us in thy grace

The Rev. Glenn Tyndall, the United Methodist campus minister at the Wesley Foundation at Virginia Tech for thirty-five years from 1974 to 2009 and my pastor for five of those years, passed away last week. In one of the photos attached to the announcement, of Glenn directing what I’m pretty sure is the 1998-99 Wesley Singers, I’m either just outside of the frame at the piano at stage left or in his shadow in the back row of the bass section. Either location would be entirely appropriate to how I fit in or sometimes didn’t over my time there – and to how Glenn made me, and everyone in and out of the frame, feel like we belonged.

Wherever life may take us as we go our separate ways
Help us share with others the things we’ve shared today

By my time, the Wesley Foundation had been well-established under his leadership for over twenty years (for much of that decidedly co-led by his wife LaVina, the heart of the place whatever her role was officially titled). Wesley served anyone who walked in the door, United Methodist or adjacent to uncertain about faith to decidedly doubtful of anything of the sort, dyed-in-the-wool liberal (him) to very much the opposite (me, at least back then), students who had a plan and followed it to those who spent several extra years finding (or not finding) what they were looking for, those intending to become pastors and those who would never consider it.

That last part was important to Glenn. The powers-that-were in United Methodism in Virginia occasionally attempted to force-rate campus ministries’ performance by a single metric: percentage of participants who proceeded directly from graduation to approved seminaries as candidates for ordination in the United Methodist Church. As he said in one of countless discussions when I’d wandered over to the Wesley building instead of doing classwork: for one thing, that was unfair at an engineering-heavy school like Tech where so many students arrived with a vocational plan already in place, compared to liberal arts schools like his beloved Duke where undergrad was perhaps more for finding direction. But more importantly, the church needed people in the pews too, and serving on committees and chaperoning the youth group and doing the hundreds of other things that keep local churches going, and we were no less critical than those considering ordained service. Everyone mattered.

May the peace of God the Father and the love of Christ his Son
Guide us in the days ahead and strengthen us each one

Every person in and out of those photos over thirty-five years had a different story that brought us to the doors at 209 West Roanoke Street. God loved us all, though, and Glenn and LaVina did too – which was not to say He, and they, didn’t sometimes need us to start acting right! Week to week, we had Sunday morning worship at Blacksburg UMC (reminding us we were part of a multi-generational Christian community even away at school), Sunday night dinners and programs, Bible studies, intramural sports, Wednesday night Wesley Singers rehearsal directed by Glenn, and Friday night social gatherings. You might say that sounds like a youth program writ large, and that was indeed his model. Developing student leadership was important to the Wesley I was part of, but I never scanned that as an explicit, forefront goal of the ministry. Sometimes presence and comfort are how you build a community, and that sense of community helps its members find their own footing from which to step out into adult faith.

And if your Singers co-chair living in the Wesley building basement discovers a 17-year-old freshman fooling around on the piano one weekend and frog-marches that kid to you to get him on the accompanists’ roster and keep him there? Well, leaders emerge in many ways.

May the blessings of the Spirit fill us from within
God bless us and return us to this fellowship once again

The Wesley Singers used Don Besig’s “A Closing Prayer”, the lyrics to which I’ve quoted here, as our closing song. We’d call alumni up to stand among the current students, sing a pretty easy piece under Glenn’s direction (although how much attention the band, and the Singers, paid to his baton would vary wildly by this stage of each show), and reaffirm our faith in the God he and LaVina served so faithfully and our membership in the community they built.

And in these coming days, God will indeed return us to this fellowship once again, this time for us to celebrate how He blessed us with Glenn’s ministry and service.

Amen.

—9 March 2026, Charlottesville, Virginia
